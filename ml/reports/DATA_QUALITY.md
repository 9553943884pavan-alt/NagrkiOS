# Data quality review

The [aggregate profile](NYC_SAMPLE_PROFILE.json) was generated from the prepared 1,000-row NYC 311 sample. All 1,000 source records had a descriptor, complaint type, agency, creation date and status. Two lack a closure date. No source-provided urgency label exists; the location field was deliberately withheld in the prepared schema even though a borough field was available. All prepared records have a Latin-script hint. This is script detection, not validated language identification.

The 1,000 rows contain only **66 distinct descriptor strings** and 934 exact repeats. There are 66 related-text groups under the current normalized text comparison. One complaint type, `Noise - Residential`, has 390 rows; many categories have just one or two. The split has 460 train, 505 validation and 35 test rows because identical descriptors stay together. The large validation share shows why a label descriptor is unsuitable as a training example or target text. A numerical 70/15/15 allocation cannot be promised when whole descriptor groups are held together.

The 998 rows with both dates produced nonnegative parseable intervals in this sample; the observed median is about 0.63 hours. This is **not** a NagrikOS resolution estimate: the sample is heavily concentrated at the start of 2024, has source-specific status semantics, and omits unresolved/censored-case treatment. No closure time, category, or urgency value was fabricated for missing records.

The export contains issue descriptors rather than citizen narratives, so encoding and narrative-language quality for Indian complaints remain unmeasured. A future corpus needs source-specific checks for malformed rows, script/language mixtures, duplicate reports, redaction misses and label validity before any training decision.

## Phase 2 synthetic sample

The [Nandi aggregate profile](NANDI_SAMPLE_PROFILE.json) covers 150 prepared generated records: 50 each of `en`, `hi`, and `hinglish`, with 45 original source categories. There are 150 distinct normalized text strings, no exact duplicates, and 150 groups under the current template-aware near-duplicate check. The split is 109 train, 16 validation and 25 test; the independent cross-collection check found no detected related group crossing a source or split boundary. All category and urgency labels are generated. The 30 `none.out_of_scope` rows are generated negative examples, not independently reviewed non-complaints.

All 150 lack creation/closure dates, department, location and status; those fields remain null. The sample has 26 P1, 37 P2 and 87 P3 generated urgency labels, which are not safety ground truth. Automated template checks cannot detect every translated or rewritten family. A small 15-ID local review sample exists, but human inspection is pending. The 2024 NYC convenience sample and Nandi quota sample should never be merged into a single population or used to claim performance on Indian grievances.

## Track A Phase 3 real source sample

The [OpenCity aggregate profile](OPENCITY_SAMPLE_PROFILE_PHASE3.json) covers 1,000 source-labeled candidate descriptions sampled across a 16,071-row official CSV. All selected descriptions meet a minimum of 25 characters and five word tokens; this is a screen for likely narratives, not human proof of citizen authorship. The source file required strict `cp1252` decoding. There are **32 original categories**, with the two largest holding 340 and 245 rows (58.5% combined); 15 categories have fewer than five rows. All 1,000 descriptions are unique under normalized exact matching, and the template-aware check found 1,000 groups with no detected near duplicates in this bounded sample. Exact-text category conflicts: 0; two source subcategory names occur under multiple parent categories and need label review. The split is 676 train, 157 validation, 167 test. A three-collection audit against NYC and Nandi found no detected cross-source or cross-split related groups.

All 1,000 rows have a `latin_script` hint and **zero human-verified language labels**. Do not call this an English-only corpus. Category-by-script candidate counts appear in [the Phase 3 matrix](PHASE3_LABEL_MATRIX.json); the matrix keeps source labels separate from NagrikOS categories. No OpenCity row has an independent complaint/non-complaint, urgency, UP routing or closure-time label. Human review is needed to determine how many records are genuinely usable.
